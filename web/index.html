One-Sixth: the first fire forecast for the Moon base

The lowest oxygen level at which a thin paper-like material keeps burning, from orbit to the Moon to Earth. Two models are fitted to NASA's reduced-gravity fire data and checked against Earth results they never saw. Only models that pass get to forecast lunar gravity in the planned habitat air (8.2 psia, 34% O2). No fire experiment has run on the Moon yet. Aircraft tests have reached lunar gravity, but only for about 20 seconds at a time.

Shaded bands: 90% range of the model. Above a band a thin cellulose sample burns (flame spreading downward in still air); below it the flame goes out.

Three Earth checks: one solid pass, one weak pass, one failure

Each model was fitted with the Earth results hidden, then asked to predict them. The pass rule was written into the code before the first run: the measured value must fall inside the 90% range and the median must be within 1.5 points.

Why the Moon is the dangerous middle

A flame needs a draft. With too little it starves, and with too much it blows off. In orbit the only draft is the fans. On the Moon, weak gravity supplies a gentle draft of its own. NASA has flagged that switching the fans off, which works on the space station, may not put a fire out there.

How the models work

Model 1 (simple curve). In still air, buoyancy drives the draft, and its speed scales as g1/3. The oxygen limit is modelled as a two-sided line in ln(g)/3: one slope for the starved side, one for the blow-off side. The step from 14.7 to 8.2 psia uses NASA White Sands test slopes.

Model 2 (physics). A flame survives while heat lost by radiation plus the time the chemistry needs stay below what the draft allows (Rrad + 1/Da < 1), the structure of the published simplified model by Takahashi and co-workers. Pressure enters through the gas density and the draft speed, so the pressure step comes from the physics.

Both are fitted by rejection sampling: draw parameters from broad ranges and keep only sets that agree with every training observation within ±0.25% O2.
